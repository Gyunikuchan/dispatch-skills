# ADR 0005: Review rounds, context, and confirmation policy

- **Status**: Accepted
- **Date**: 2026-09-29
- **Updated**: 2026-11-28
- **Audience**: Maintainers

## Context

Read-only reviewers do not share the orchestrator's chat history, and a diff or commit message alone cannot establish requested outcomes or settled user decisions. At the same time, treating the reviewed artifact's own decisions as settled or anchoring every voice on a shared context map upfront causes independent models to make correlated errors.

During review rounds, a hard round cap bounds cost but cannot guarantee a blocking defect is resolved, while an unconfirmed orchestrator rejection can repeat the orchestrator's own blind spot. Conversely, unbounded MUST rounds mostly chase fix-induced regressions, and the churn controls alone did not stop them: two later plan reviews had reviewers tag 13 of 19 and 17 of 27 findings after round 1 as fix-induced. Tight uniform idle timeouts caused false stops when streaming delegates paused inside a reasoning step (up to 137 s observed inside a step versus no silent gaps above 60 s between steps).

## Decision

### Intent and context composition

- **Spec-first reading:** Separate governing intent ([ADR 0010](0010-canonical-session-intent.md)) from implementation evidence. Reviewers read the full governing spec first. They treat the spec's Decisions and Non-goals—and decisions in an approved parent design or plan other than the artifact under review—as settled, never the reviewed artifact's own decisions.
- **Pre-registration and context maps:** In round 1 of a plan or design review, reviewers pre-register 3–5 expected changes and risks from the spec and touched code before opening the artifact or context map. The context map is a pre-change locator (omitted from code review, where it is stale), not a completeness claim; reviewers verify claims at source and run their own consumer/caller searches. Quality-family bullet order rotates across slots so independence comes from model diversity rather than prompt slicing.
- **Holistic voices:** Reviews use whole-target voices across providers rather than diff-sliced micro-reviewers so each voice retains cross-file architectural context.

### Rounds, confirmation, and fix verification

- **Caps, continuation, and ceiling:** One policy governs plan, design, and code review ([review rules](../../skills/dispatch/references/review-rules.md)). Routine quality rounds stop at the saved round-1 cap, while accepted MUST fixes and pending MUST rejections continue up to the ceiling (`cap + 2`, plus 2 per user grant) before escalating through the [review escalation Decision Frame](../../skills/dispatch/references/decision-frame.md#review-escalation).
- **Rejected MUST confirmation:** A rejected MUST requires explicit agreement from the original logical source slot (or configured reserve/native substitute) so unilateral orchestrator rejection cannot silence an independent check. When no decisive finding or target move requires a full/narrowed round, a compact confirmation-only prompt checks pending MUST rejections at minimal token cost. Repeated disagreement or non-response escalates to the user.
- **Inter-round context and fix review:** Re-reviews receive inter-round comparisons and `### Settled in earlier rounds` (with clipped defect/rationale lines and `(new)` markers) to judge `origin` (`original`, `fix-induced`, `unknown`). Every accepted plan or design fix batch (including final-round SHOULD fixes) runs an advisory `brief fix-review-lite` explorer before the next round or report; code and integration fixes use the fixer's consumer self-check.
- **Churn controls before the next round:** Fixers (review rules step 4) name the tests, golden fixtures and byte or line ceilings that pin the text they change, with current headroom, and list and re-check the consumer of each qualifier, branch or field a fix deletes or narrows; the fix-review-lite explorer runs the same consumer search on plan and design batches. Plan authors and reviewers apply the quality-bar `Pins:` bullet, so each task names its pins and owns every golden, generated output or ceiling its change can exceed. These controls target byte-ceiling headroom defects and fix-induced regressions. They cannot act on a round that churns for another cause, so the step-back below acts on the round itself.
- **Proposed fix and recorded fix choice:** The reviewer's remedy is `proposedFix` (formerly `requiredChange`, "Required change"), shown as "Proposed fix" and described to reviewers as a proposal the author may replace; `defect` states what must stop being true. Each `accepted` review ruling carries `data.fix`: `proposed`, or `alternative: <reason>`. The orchestrator chooses `proposed` only when the fix removes the cause for every failing state, is the smallest change that does so, sits at the right document layer and leaves each consumer holding. `ledger append` refuses an accepted review ruling without a fix choice (`invalid-record`, naming `data.fix`) and checks presence only, never the reason text. One field follows the prefix precedent of the ceiling resolutions (`re-author:`, `relax-split:`). The cause: the old label presented the remedy as an obligation, and no ruling could record that the orchestrator chose a different fix. Findings saved before this change lack `proposedFix`; `review run` refuses them with `stale-findings` before it writes anything, so a review finishes on the Dispatch version that started it. There is no migration.
- **Step-back trigger and outcome:** From round 2, `review run` saves `stepBack` in `findings.json` when either condition holds: a unit has `hotStreak` of at least 2 (the marker lists those units), or at least two findings of the round and more than half of the round's findings are fix-induced (the marker holds `[count, total]`). The `count >= 2` floor keeps a round with one fix-induced finding from firing the share condition. The summary then states `step-back due` and `rulings.json` carries a `step-back:<review>:<round>` skeleton. `ledger append` refuses a batch that holds an accepted ruling of that round while neither the batch nor the ledger holds a ruling with that subject. An absent or unreadable `findings.json` imposes no requirement, so a damaged file never stops rulings. The outcome is an ordinary `ruling` record (no new ledger kind), with `patch`, `rewrite`, `re-author` or `user` and a nonblank rationale; review-ruling readers match `review:<review>:`, so it changes no findings count, confirmation or admission. The [step-back procedure](../../skills/dispatch/references/review-rules.md#step-back-procedure) holds the rule content.
- **Authority change:** From round 2 the orchestrator decides `patch`, `rewrite` and `re-author` of a plan or design without the user. Previously re-authoring was offered only at the review ceiling, and the shared-root-cause and hot-unit rewrite rules were prose that neither run followed (1 of 73 accepted plan rulings cited a shared root cause; no ledger recorded a whole-unit rewrite or re-author). `user` covers relaxing or splitting a requirement and moving a plan up to a design through the review escalation Decision Frame; discarding integrated code stays a user gate. The ceiling (`cap + 2`, plus 2 per user grant) and its resolutions are unchanged and remain the last resort. Voice count, roster, round-1 cap and rejection confirmation do not change.
- **Evidence and limits:** Two plan reviews ran on controllers that already had the churn controls. One (cap 3, 5 rounds) had 13 of 19 findings after round 1 tagged fix-induced, and its rounds 4 and 5 held fix-induced findings only. The other, the diagnostics-fixes run (cap 2, 4 rounds), had 17 of 27. An explorer classed 9 of 13 fix-induced findings in the first run as one fix changing a section and leaving another inconsistent, and 3 more as one guard extended one path per round; in the second run, fixes added mechanisms whose unhandled states the next round found, and the first fix batch replaced 20 KB of plan text with 27 KB. The trigger was tuned on the saved rounds of the diagnostics-fixes run. Its plan review is marked at round 2 (fix-induced share 5 of 8) and at round 3 (units with `hotStreak` 2, and 9 of 10 fix-induced); its code-integration review converged with 3, 1 and 0 findings and is never marked. Limits: the saved first run is currently unavailable, so its per-round marker trace was not re-derived; the reviewer `origin` tag under-counts fix-induced findings, and the hot-unit condition covers the gap; no design review ran on a controller with these controls, so a design review is assumed to behave like a plan review; code-review fix diffs were not retained, so code lineage is unverified.
- **Round cause as measurement:** Each review round after round 1 saves `cause` (`primary` and partitioned `counts`) in `findings.json`, and `diagnostics render` lists each round past the saved cap by cause or `unrecorded`. The cause changes no admission, roster, cap, ceiling or round count.

### Phase-aware idle limits and session resume

- Streaming CLI delegates that emit step events stop after 60 s of silence between steps and 180 s inside a step (unclassified lines default to the longer limit); other streaming delegates stop after 120 s, and Claude and Antigravity are never idle-stopped. Stopped or unfinished CLI slots resume in their saved session within the resume limit ([provider controls](../../skills/dispatch/references/providers.md#shared-provider-controls), [review contract](../../skills/dispatch/references/toolkit.md#review-run)).

## Consequences

Reviewers share durable intent with authors and writers while resisting framing bias through round-1 pre-registration. Blocking defects and disputed MUST rejections get independent closure or escalate cleanly at the ceiling, and phase-aware idle timeouts eliminate false mid-step kills.
