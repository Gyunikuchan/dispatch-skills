# Review rules

Keep findings, sources, fixes, duplicates, revision, scope and rulings in linked artifacts. The toolkit refreshes bound plan/design findings after each completed round, ruling or post-review bind. Walkthroughs derive findings from the ledger. Keep resolution rationale in rulings and leave generated sections to the toolkit. Retain a full standalone report. Inspect raw provider output when evidence is disputed.

Review prompts carry the settled governing spec path and hash. Plan and design review prompts also name the session context map as a pre-change locator, not a completeness claim, when it exists; code review prompts never receive it, because the pre-change map is stale for code. Round-1 plan and design reviewers pre-register expectations before opening the artifact or map. Every reviewer reads the whole spec. Inspect the relevant host standards and [quality bar](templates/quality-bar.md); the [shared review prompt](templates/review-prompt.md) owns adjacent discovery.

## Intent and findings

Read `findings.md` before adjudication. Check intent and spec findings first against REQs and DECs. Use spec for governing-text defects and testing for missing verification. Verify every claim against current source and governing outcomes; accept demonstrated defects regardless of vote counts. Locate a finding marked `unresolved-locus:` from its defect text before ruling; it never matches by location. Rate findings with the shared [severity rubric](templates/review-severity.md). Reject a maintainability or performance finding against a writer-reported shortcut after verifying its stated limit, citing the report, unless the expected load crosses that limit. A shortcut that removes a simplicity-floor protection is a defect, not a shortcut.

A settled DEC may resolve an intent claim autonomously: cite it in the rationale. Cases that the [user gate](decision-frame.md#user-gate-boundaries) lists go to the user. Record changed intent through [spec operation](spec-stage.md#revision-protocol). For an accepted unrequested-behaviour `intent` finding, the orchestrator removes the behaviour; keeping it changes intent and requires a recorded user quote through the [user gate](decision-frame.md#user-gate-boundaries).

## Review provenance and applicability

Retain reviewed revision, scope, findings, rulings and failed or missing coverage. After edits, choose continued coverage from changed paths, accepted fixes and disputed claims; never attribute newer bytes to an earlier review.

A round `n > 1` runs confirmation-only when `!rerun`, `!coverage`, `!decisive` (no accepted `MUST` and no accepted `SHOULD` below `cap1` in the latest substantive round), `same(prevRound, captured)`, and `requests.length > 0`; it resolves only pending `MUST` rejection requests with a compact prompt ([review run](toolkit.md#review-run)). Any decisive finding (an accepted `MUST`, or an accepted `SHOULD` from a round below `cap1`), coverage round, granted rerun or moved target restores the standard full or narrowed prompt.

Level policy selects roster and round caps. The cap bounds quality rounds; every round saves the round-1 cap, so a later level change moves neither the cap nor the ceiling. An accepted MUST fix or a pending MUST rejection earns continuation past the saved round-1 cap up to the ceiling: the round-1 cap + 2 + 2 per user-resolved grant. A `review-ceiling` refusal opens `ceiling:<review>:<ceiling>` and blocks new rounds until a user-quoted resolution records `re-author:`, `model-effort:` or `relax-split:` for 2 more rounds, or `close:` or `defer:` for zero additional quality rounds. Route that refusal through the [review escalation Decision Frame](decision-frame.md#review-escalation) and preserve its resolution record. Result and status `budget` ([review run](toolkit.md#review-run)) only reports, using the launch policy: it opens no escalation and grants no admission or resolution, and an unruled MUST is not an accepted continuation.

After round 1, `review run` sets the round roster at round start. It launches the full configured roster when the previous completed round has an accepted MUST, or an accepted SHOULD from a round below the saved round-1 cap. Otherwise it launches one target: the first non-reserve slot of the previous round's saved roster; configured reserves stay as fallbacks. Label a re-review `(full)` when selection keeps the configured primary roster, or `(narrowed)` when it selects the previous round's first target. In a `code-integration` review, an open slot replaces that target and the label is `(coverage)`; see [failed-slot coverage](review-coverage.md#failed-slot-coverage). All completed forms satisfy delivery identity. Slots that own pending MUST rejection requests stay assigned and do not change the primary-roster label.

For the final completed review round at or above the saved round-1 cap, apply accepted SHOULD findings from that round and record each correction with its current verification. This also covers the final round after a MUST-driven continuation. For plan and design batches, run step 8 before the report. These fixes need no review solely for themselves; keep the completed review as the reviewed baseline and report corrections as verified but not re-reviewed. A finding corrected from a higher rating to CONSIDER counts as SHOULD here, because its fix stays required. Use this exception only for recorded fixes of accepted SHOULD findings from that round; unrelated edits and unverified fixes require review. Accepted MUST fixes still require review unless the user resolves the ceiling with `close:`. Apply accepted fixes under `close:` and record any un-re-reviewed fixes. Pending MUST rejections still require confirmation.

Load [failed-slot coverage](review-coverage.md#failed-slot-coverage) only on a slot failure, and [repository changes](review-coverage.md#repository-changes-from-native-voices) only when a round reports `repositoryChanges`.

## Rulings and fix scope

Fill `rulings.json` with `accepted`, `rejected`, `duplicate` or `deferred` and evidence-backed rationales. `accepted` rulings also take `fix` (step 2). Append the array in one `ledger append`. The template fills `review:<review>:<finding>` subjects and `ruling:<review>:<round>:<finding>` keys. For a later ruling of that finding, add `:<m>` to its initial key, counting from 2.

Correct a wrong rating on an `accepted` ruling with corrected `impact` and `trigger` under the same rubric; the rationale names each changed dimension. The fix stays required and no reviewer confirmation is needed. A current `critical` cannot be lowered by correction: use the rejection route. A correction to `critical` names the boundary crossed or the data lost in its rationale. A later ruling without values leaves the correction in force. A rejected finding takes the higher of its original and latest corrected severities. A downgrade appears in the walkthrough attention list. Reject repeated settled claims without new evidence by citing the prior ruling. Before accepting a finding that reverses an earlier accepted ruling or an approved plan criterion, cite the governing spec or plan in the ruling and resolve the conflict there.

A rejected MUST remains pending until each original logical source slot, or its configured reserve/native substitute, explicitly agrees with an evidence-backed rationale. For a tool-only finding, use the first non-reserve reviewer in its original saved roster; if none exists, stop with `review-confirmation-required`. The next review sends each slot its own captured requests. When the [confirmation-only predicate](#review-provenance-and-applicability) holds, that review is the compact round. Missing, malformed, failed or unattributed replies leave rejection pending; omission from ordinary findings is not agreement. SHOULD and CONSIDER rejections need no confirmation.

A second consecutive explicit disagreement, or a second consecutive completed round past the cap without a valid attributed reply from an assigned source for a pending rejection, opens `rejection:<review>:<finding>` and blocks review launches in that series. Count no-response attempts per original finding and assigned logical source across target and ruling edits. Agreement resets the disagreement count; a valid reply resets only that source's no-response count. Failed replies reset neither count, and target/rationale edits do not resolve escalation.

Escalate through the [Decision Frame](decision-frame.md#review-escalation); retain the user decision evidence in a resolved escalation ledger record before retrying. Resolution re-arms confirmation, retains the disagreement count, and starts a new bounded no-response attempt. The next explicit disagreement can reopen escalation. Accepting the finding for repair removes the rejection obligation.

Delivery and report completion require confirmed applicable MUST rejections. Bound plan/design and current integration-base reviews retain obligations across content edits and disabled phases. Duplicate rulings cannot close a pending original. Later target edits do not reopen a confirmed unchanged ruling. Rejection of the same finding under a new ruling needs fresh confirmation.

Route accepted fixes within governing scope: plan and design fixes go to the orchestrator. In implementation integration, a mechanical correction confirmed by a deterministic check goes to utility first. Route a remaining fix to the responsible task writer if it changes multiple files, an executable production statement, a production configuration or schema directive that changes runtime behaviour, or a test assertion; the orchestrator handles remaining small fixes. A content-preserving rename counts as one file when the diff contains only that rename; an importer edit counts as another file. Standalone review uses direct fixes by the orchestrator. Follow the [integration assignment procedure](review-fixes.md) for writer and utility fixes. Identify every source, test, fixture, helper and generated path needed before editing. Verify fixes and record the resulting evidence.

The orchestrator runs steps 1–3, 7 and 8; each fixer runs steps 4–6.

1. Rule every finding in the round before the first edit.
2. An accepted ruling accepts the defect, cites its governing REQ, DEC, AD or code contract, and judges the proposed fix. Set `fix: proposed` when the fix removes the cause for every failing state, is the smallest change that does so, sits at the right document layer, and leaves each consumer of the touched text or code holding; else set `fix: alternative: <reason>`. The choice sets direction; steps 4–6 stay with the fixer.
3. Look for a cause shared across finding groups, then state one root cause per group. Resolve each group as one coherent change, ordered by actual interfaces. Fix a defective predicate so every failing state passes, not only the listed case.
4. Before changing text or code, check why it exists and who uses it. Prefer narrowing or removal; if the fix grows its unit, state why neither meets the requirement. Name the tests, golden fixtures and byte or line ceilings that pin it, with current headroom. For each qualifier, branch or field the fix deletes or narrows, list the consumer that relied on it (in the reply or the `edit` rationale) and confirm it still holds before the next round.
5. Fix at the artifact level. Spec states intent and acceptance. Design states architectural contracts and invariants. Plan states tactics, tasks, touched interfaces and verification. Code supplies routine implementation mechanisms. Resolve a request for design mechanism with a contract and route the tactics to the plan.
6. Before writing an added or changed claim about existing code or docs, confirm at source that it supports the claim; for data production or consumption, find the code that writes or reads it. Write each verified fact the fix depends on into the artifact.
7. Read `findings.json` `units` and `stepBack`. Rewrite a hot unit, or step back when the summary states `step-back due`, under the [step-back procedure](#step-back-procedure).
8. After each accepted plan or design fix batch, including final-round SHOULD fixes, run a lightweight fix review before the next round or report. Without a launch tool, run the brief inline, record mode `inline` in the note and mark the review inline in the verb report (the plan or design handoff).
   - Record the edit and render the brief via `lint run --edit --fix-review-lite`; launch it on its `launchAs`.
   - Verify each claim at source and fix or rule it before the next round or report. Reject only with source evidence that the requirement holds. Repeat the review once at most per batch, only when the batch fixed an accepted MUST finding, with `--fix-review-lite` on the further `lint run --edit`.
   - After claims settle, record one `note` per batch, including any repeat. Copy the summary skeleton with its key (`fix-review-lite:<review>:<round>:<n>`, `n` the stable, unique one-based batch ordinal within that review round) and fill the claim counts, rejected claims with source evidence, and mode. The review is advisory. Send it with the next `review run --note`, or `ledger append` it before the report after a final batch.

For code and integration fixes, before the next review round, whoever applied accepted fixes re-reads the changed sections or paths together with the other consumers of each touched contract, and resolves conflicts before the round; this self-check launches no subagent and changes no roster, cap or round rule.

Adjacent work and optional findings need user opt-in when outside approved scope; preserve unselected findings as follow-ups. In-scope acceptance supplies opt-in only for the selected bounded work. A recurring finding after an accepted fix follows the [step-back procedure](#step-back-procedure).

Malformed, empty, truncated or refused provider reports use [provider fallback](providers.md). Unknown nonempty tags remain usable as uncategorized with originalTag; do not silently drop their claims. A failed fix or failed check stays open with logs and options.

### Step-back procedure

Run this procedure when the `review run` summary states `step-back due` or a finding recurs after an accepted fix. `rulings.json` then holds a `step-back:<review>:<round>` skeleton; `ledger append` refuses the round's accepted rulings until a filled outcome is in the batch or the ledger. The orchestrator decides from round 2 without the user, except where `user` below applies.

1. Look for a cause shared by the round's fix-induced findings and hot units: one fact stated in several places; a fix that added a mechanism the artifact never grounded; a requirement that the fixes cannot satisfy; content at the wrong document layer.
2. Record one outcome as a `ruling` with subject `step-back:<review>:<round>` and a one-line `rationale`:
   - `patch`: continue patching. State the evidence that the cause is local or the review is converging. A repeated `patch` in one review states what changed since the previous step-back. A unit rewritten in the previous round reads as hot again; a `patch` that names the rewrite is valid.
   - `rewrite`: rewrite the named units whole; the rationale names them. Each rewritten unit is derived from its governing requirements and the accepted defects, not from the patch history, and cites its task, criterion, REQ, DEC, AD or interface IDs. Search the artifact for those IDs.
   - `re-author`: re-author the plan or design the same way.
   - `user`: take the decision through the [Decision Frame](decision-frame.md#review-escalation). It covers relaxing or splitting a requirement, moving a plan up to a design, and re-authoring code. Discarding integrated code stays a [user gate](decision-frame.md#user-gate-boundaries).
3. The next round reviews a rewritten unit or re-authored artifact under the existing admission rules. At a final round with no accepted MUST, no round follows: choose `patch` or `user`.
